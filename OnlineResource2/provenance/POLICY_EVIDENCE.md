# Policy-date evidence checked on 30 September 2026

## January 2017

The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism's Weekly Gonggam, issue 387, is dated 9 January 2017 on its cover and publication information page. Printed page 36 (PDF page 38) labels the Seoul-wide old-diesel restriction as implemented in January 2017. The accompanying paragraph describes expansion from geographically narrower roads to all Seoul. This supports the monthly rollout assignment, not first-ever exposure to a diesel restriction or uniform camera activation.

[Government publication](https://www.korea.kr/goNewsRes/attaches/2019.05/23/107_20170109_01IozeNoYDGJM000.pdf). The 2019 path is a web-upload path, not the issue date. The web tool could extract the publication and policy text but could not render a screenshot; the citation relies on the extracted text.

## July 2018

MECAR's permanent old-diesel restriction page states integrated operation from 1 July 2018 across Seoul, Incheon and 17 Gyeonggi cities. It separately describes the three annual rollout phases. The study does not reset Seoul to July 2018. The second-phase January 2018 alternative tests earlier administrative rollout; July is not asserted to be the first local action in every district.

[MECAR permanent restrictions](https://www.mecar.or.kr/dr/info/oldDieselCarAlwaysDr.do).

## January 2020

The 4 August 2016 government announcement supports the final geographic expansion in 2020. MECAR describes coverage extending to 28 Gyeonggi cities by that year. Neither reviewed primary source establishes a shared first-enforcement month across the 11 additional cities. January remains an assumption. July and December are sensitivity scenarios within the announced year, not newly verified dates. No district-level camera or penalty history was supplied.

[Ministry of Environment announcement](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148819495).

## Analysis rule

Each scenario changes one phase at a time; the sample, weather, pollutants, spatial matrix and controls are unchanged. Cohort-trend membership is fixed independently of the recoded month. Second-phase recoding changes 210 district-month indicators; third-phase July and December recodings change 78 and 143. All five outcomes are reported with and without trends. This does not validate parallel trends, recover enforcement intensity, or address every possible implementation history.
